# Object Translation Hard Cases

Read this file only when a Level 1 or important Level 2 non-floral object is present. These are cross-topic translation patterns, not visual templates. Preserve the source object's useful visual properties; never reproduce the object itself.

## Universal gate

```text
Source object
→ extract color / material / texture / form / emotion
→ assign those properties to flower / wrapper / ribbon / special material / branch or line
→ verify that the original entity is no longer recognizable
```

Use this priority: color correspondence, then material/texture, then form/direction, then emotion. Literal entity preservation is the lowest-priority option and is prohibited for ordinary props.

## Case 1 — Scarf or fabric item

Extract:

- dominant and secondary colors;
- softness, weave, fibers, translucency, or sheen;
- flowing, folded, trailing, or wrapped motion;
- warmth, intimacy, ease, restraint, or theatricality.

Translate into:

- corresponding flower colors or source-justified dyed floral support;
- fibrous, textile-like, translucent, or softly folded wrapper layers;
- ribbon or fine linear material carrying the flow direction;
- small flowers or atmosphere materials repeating the cloth rhythm.

Prohibit a real scarf, wearable knot, recognizable scarf tails, copied fringe, or a complete cloth silhouette. The result must read as floral color and textile behavior, not “a scarf placed in a bouquet.”

## Case 2 — Headwear or accessory

Extract:

- scale, delicacy, shine, transparency, and edge quality;
- curved, radial, linear, clustered, or suspended geometry;
- repeated beads, links, points, or small accents;
- refined, playful, ceremonial, restrained, or handmade emotion.

Translate into:

- delicate small flowers or compact secondary events;
- thin branches, wire, grass, or other linear decoration;
- restrained metallic, pearlescent, reflective, or translucent details;
- rhythm and placement derived from the accessory rather than its silhouette.

Prohibit copied headwear, jewelry, glasses, crowns, clips, wearable loops, or miniature accessories. Metallic or pearlescent detail is a material behavior, not permission to rebuild the object.

## Case 3 — Conical hat or woven bamboo object

Extract:

- straw, tan, kraft, or warm natural-fiber color;
- dry, matte, handmade, interlaced texture;
- radial, lattice, curved, or planar geometry;
- rural, practical, quiet, sunlit, or handcrafted emotion.

Translate into:

- woven-texture paper, handmade paper, kraft paper, or natural-fiber wrapper;
- broken abstract lattice, restrained radial fold, or layered planar geometry;
- natural-fiber tie, mesh, fine branch rhythm, or dry structural accent;
- warm neutral support colors around the floral body.

Prohibit a recognizable conical hat, crown-and-brim silhouette, basket, fan, or literal bamboo prop. A radial or woven cue must remain partial and abstract so the output reads as bouquet structure and packaging.

## Acceptance check

For every case, the bouquet should retain the source object's important color, material, motion/form, and emotional contribution while eliminating its literal identity. If a viewer can name the original prop from a copied silhouette rather than infer its qualities from the floral design, translation has failed.
